The short version
  • We only collect what an order or an account needs.
  • Guest orders belong to the restaurant, not to us.
  • We never sell personal information.
  • We analyze public information about your restaurant, and your menu on Dinelabs, to give you insights.
  • You can ask for a copy, a fix, or deletion anytime.

1. Who we are and what this covers

Dinelabs (“Dinelabs”, “we”, “us”) provides online storefronts that let restaurants and coffee shops take orders directly from their guests. We are headquartered in Beirut, Lebanon, and our team works from Beirut and Tbilisi. As we grow, we may also operate through affiliated companies or branches in other countries, for example in Saudi Arabia. “Dinelabs” means the Dinelabs company that provides the Services to you, which your invoice or the portal names, together with its affiliates. You can reach us at team@dinelabs.co.

This policy explains what personal information we collect, how we use it, and the choices you have. It applies to everything we offer, now and in the future (together, the “Services”):

  • our website, dinelabs.co
  • the storefronts we host for restaurants, including ordering by QR code at tables
  • the Dinelabs Manager portal restaurants use to manage menus, orders, customers, settings and plans
  • our mobile apps, and any new product or feature we launch, such as loyalty, online payments or menu analysis

If a new product collects information in a way this policy doesn’t describe, we’ll update this policy before that product launches.

2. Who this policy is for

  • Merchants: restaurants and coffee shops that sign up for Dinelabs, and the people who run their accounts
  • Guests: people who view a menu or place an order through a Merchant’s storefront
  • Visitors: people who browse our website
  • Business contacts: people at a restaurant who talk to us before or while signing up, for example to book a demo or to message us on WhatsApp

3. Our role

Merchant account data. When you sign up as a Merchant, we decide how your account information is used. For this data we are the data controller. The same goes for website visitors and business contacts.

Guest order data. When a guest orders from a restaurant, the order belongs to the restaurant. The restaurant decides how to use it to prepare, deliver and follow up on that order, and is responsible for it as the data controller. We process that data on the restaurant’s behalf to run the storefront and deliver the order to them. If you’re a guest and have a question about how a restaurant uses your information, contact that restaurant first. We’ll help if they can’t.

Insights we create. The analyses, benchmarks and aggregated data we build (see sections 6 and 7) are ours, and we are responsible for them.

4. What we collect

We only collect what we need to run the Services.

From Merchants

  • Account details: your name, email address and password, your phone or WhatsApp number if you give it to us, and the branches you manage
  • Restaurant details: restaurant and brand name, storefront link, country, currency, time zone, address and map pin, opening hours, delivery zones, tables, and social media links
  • Menu and store content: products, prices, categories, add-ons, labels, descriptions, nutrition details, photos, logos, colors and translations you add
  • Imports: website links, PDF files and photos you give us so we can import your menu
  • Orders and customers: the orders your storefront receives and the customer details attached to them, which you see in your orders, history and customers pages
  • Plan and billing: your plan, trial dates, invoices, payment history, and the payment method you choose (cash, bank transfer, Whish, OMT or card). We never store full card numbers
  • Online payment setup: if you connect an online payment account, the business and bank details the payment provider needs to verify you, such as your business name, bank account (IBAN) and owner ID details
  • Notification settings: the email address, Telegram connection or WhatsApp number where you want new-order alerts
  • Security and device details: sign-in times, device and browser type, and the approximate city, so you can see where your account is signed in and sign out other devices
  • Usage data: how you use the portal, such as logins, settings and menu changes, and how many times products on your storefront are viewed and ordered
  • Messages: anything you send us by email, WhatsApp or other channels, including the store, branch, page and plan details a support message includes

From Guests

Guests don’t need an account to order. We only ask for what the order needs:

  • Dine-in orders: the table number, the items ordered, add-ons and order notes
  • Pickup orders: the items ordered and the contact details needed to reach you about the order
  • Delivery orders: your name, phone number, email address and delivery location, plus the items ordered and any notes
  • Payment details for the order: whether you pay by cash or card, the tip, and whether the payment went through. Card details go straight to the payment provider and not to us
  • Promo codes you enter, and the language you choose to view the menu in

From everyone who uses the Services

  • Technical data: IP address, device and browser type, pages viewed and the time of your visit
  • Cookies and similar technologies: see section 12

From public and third-party sources

  • Public information about your restaurant and about similar places nearby, such as business listings, ratings, opening hours, photos, prices and menus published online. Section 6 explains how we use it
  • Information from our providers, for example whether a payment succeeded or whether a message was delivered

6. Analyzing your restaurant and your menu

Part of what Dinelabs does is help you make better decisions with real numbers. To do that, we analyze information about your restaurant. This section tells you exactly what we look at.

What we may analyze

  • Publicly available information about your restaurant. For example, your Google listing and other public business profiles: name, address and location, category, opening hours, photos, ratings and reviews. Also your public website and social media pages, and public listings on review or delivery platforms
  • Public information about similar places nearby, such as their prices, menus and ratings, so we can compare your restaurant with its surroundings
  • Your menu on Dinelabs. Categories, products, prices, descriptions, photos, labels, add-ons, availability and translations
  • How your storefront performs. Product views, orders, order totals and top sellers, in aggregated form

What we use it for

  • menu analysis: your top sellers, how product views turn into orders, and menu quality checks such as missing photos or descriptions
  • price comparisons, for example how your prices compare with similar places nearby
  • location insights about your restaurant and the area around it
  • suggestions that help you sell more, such as add-ons that go well with a dish
  • building and improving our benchmarks, models and the Services themselves, including products we launch later

The ground rules

  • We only use information that is publicly available. We don’t log in to anyone’s private accounts or get around any access restriction.
  • Insights about your restaurant are shown to you and your team. When we use your data to help other Merchants, it is combined with data from many restaurants and doesn’t identify you (see section 7).
  • These analyses rely on your menu, prices and aggregated sales figures. They don’t use guests’ personal details.
  • Public information can be incomplete or out of date, so treat insights as guidance and not as a promise.

Your choice

You can ask us to stop using public information about your restaurant, or to delete the analyses we built about it, by emailing team@dinelabs.co. We’ll consider your request and honor it where the law requires. Insights depend on this information, so stopping may switch off menu analysis for your account. Aggregated data that no longer identifies you can stay.

7. Aggregated and anonymized data

We combine information across many restaurants, such as menu items, prices, categories and order volumes, and remove anything that identifies a person or a single restaurant. We use this aggregated data to build insights like menu analysis and price benchmarks, and to improve the Services.

Aggregated data never identifies a guest. It never identifies a Merchant to other Merchants unless that Merchant agrees. Because it no longer identifies anyone, it isn’t personal information.

8. AI, translation and other automated tools

Some features use automated tools, some of them run by outside providers:

  • Translation of menus into other languages, and of orders and order notes into the language your kitchen reads
  • Menu import from a website link, a PDF or a photo. We read out categories, products, prices and descriptions, and show them to you on a review screen before anything is saved
  • Picture enhancement, such as cleaning up a product photo or changing its background. You can keep the result or go back to the original
  • Suggestions and insights, such as the analysis described in section 6

To make these work, we send the relevant content, such as menu text, product photos, order notes or imported files, to our providers, under contracts that limit how they can use it. Automated results can contain mistakes, so please check them. We don’t use these tools to make decisions about a person that have legal or similarly significant effects.

9. Who we share information with

We share information only as described here:

  • The restaurant you order from: your order and the details needed to prepare, deliver and contact you about it
  • Service providers that help us run Dinelabs, under contracts that limit how they can use your data:
    • Contabo, which hosts our servers and databases
    • Cloudflare, which stores and delivers images and helps protect our website
    • Google, whose business listings and maps we use for restaurant and location information, and whose other services we use to run our business
    • translation and AI providers, which process menus, imported files, pictures and order notes for the features in section 8
    • payment providers, including Whish and the card payment providers a Merchant connects, which process payments and subscription payments
    • messaging and email providers, including WhatsApp and Telegram, which deliver order alerts, support messages and service messages
    • HubSpot, which we use to keep track of sales and onboarding conversations with business contacts
  • Advertising and analytics providers: Meta (the Meta Pixel), Google Ads and Microsoft Clarity, which help us measure our ads and see how visitors use our website
  • Legal and safety reasons: when the law requires it, or to protect the rights, safety or property of our users, the public or Dinelabs
  • Business changes: if Dinelabs is merged, acquired or sells its assets, information may transfer to the new owner, who must respect this policy

When we add a new provider, for example for online payments or our mobile apps, we’ll add them to this list.

10. International transfers

Our providers may store or process information outside your country, including in Europe and the United States, and our team works from Lebanon, Georgia and the other countries where we operate. When information leaves your country, we take reasonable steps to make sure it stays protected as this policy describes.

12. Cookies and similar technologies

We use a small number of cookies and similar technologies:

  • Essential cookies keep you logged in, remember your language, and hold your order while you browse a menu. The Services don’t work without them.
  • Analytics tools, such as Microsoft Clarity, help us understand how people use our website, which can include recordings of how visitors click and scroll, so we can improve it. Where the law requires, we ask for your consent first.
  • Advertising cookies, such as the Meta Pixel and Google Ads, help us measure how our ads perform. Where the law requires, we ask for your consent first.

You can withdraw consent at any time by clearing the cookies for our website in your browser, or by emailing us. You can also block or delete cookies in your browser settings. If you block essential cookies, ordering may not work.

13. How we protect it

We use encrypted connections (HTTPS), access controls, and limits on who in our team can see personal information. Our team’s access depends on their role, two-step login is required for our most privileged roles, and changes made in our admin tools are recorded. In the portal, you can see where your account is signed in and sign out of other devices.

No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your information, we’ll tell you and the relevant authorities as the law requires.

14. Your rights and choices

You can ask us to:

  • give you a copy of the personal information we hold about you
  • correct information that’s wrong
  • delete your information
  • stop or limit how we use it, including for analysis (section 6) and for product news
  • withdraw consent you gave earlier
  • give you your menu and order data in a file you can take with you

You can also change your notification settings and sign out of devices in the portal, and unsubscribe from product news with the link in any of those emails.

Email us at team@dinelabs.co. We’ll reply within 30 days and may ask you to confirm your identity first. If you’re a guest asking about an order, we’ll work with the restaurant, which controls that data, to handle your request. You can also contact your local data protection authority if you’re not happy with how we handled your request.

15. Children

The Services aren’t aimed at children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect their information. Merchant accounts are for adults who can enter into a contract for a business. If you believe a child has given us personal information, contact us and we’ll delete it.

16. Changes to this policy

We’ll update this policy as Dinelabs grows, for example when we launch mobile apps, loyalty or online payments. We’ll post the new version here and change the “Last updated” date. If a change is significant, we’ll email Merchants or show a notice in the portal at least 15 days before it takes effect.
